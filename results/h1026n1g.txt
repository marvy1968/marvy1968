### 1. Five Multi-Day Swing Ideas (Stocks & ETFs, Daily Bars)

**Idea 1: Post-Earnings Announcement Drift (PEAD) with a Gap Filter**
*   **Rule:** Buy at the close of the earnings day IF the stock gapped up > 3% from the previous close AND closed higher than its open; hold for 10 days.
*   **Inefficiency:** Analysts and institutional models anchor to old data and take days/weeks to fully revise their price targets, causing a slow drift in the direction of the surprise.
*   **Data Needed:** Daily bars, earnings dates for the last 10 years.
*   **Kill Test:** The strategy generates a Profit Factor (PF) < 1.1 on a 5-year out-of-sample set after applying 3 bps slippage per side.

**Idea 2: 52-Week High Breakout on High Relative Volume**
*   **Rule:** Buy on a daily close that sets a new 252-day high IF daily volume is > 2x the 50-day average volume; exit on a trailing 10-day low.
*   **Inefficiency:** The "Disposition Effect." Retail investors sell winners too early to lock in gains, artificially capping the price. Once that supply is absorbed (indicated by high volume), the stock runs free.
*   **Data Needed:** Daily price and volume.
*   **Kill Test:** A control strategy of buying *every* 52-week high (regardless of volume) outperforms the volume-filtered variant. 

**Idea 3: Sector ETF Relative Momentum (Dual Momentum)**
*   **Rule:** Every 20 days, rank 11 sector ETFs by their 120-day return. Buy the top 3 IF their 120-day return is > 0 AND SPY's 120-day return is > 0; hold for 20 days.
*   **Inefficiency:** Macro capital rotation. Large funds cannot move billions into a sector in one day; their buying persists over weeks/months.
*   **Data Needed:** Daily closes for SPY and the 11 Select Sector SPDRs (XLF, XLK, etc.).
*   **Kill Test:** Buying and holding equal-weight SPY outperforms the rotation strategy on a risk-adjusted basis (Sharpe/Max Drawdown) over a 10-year test.

**Idea 4: Turn-of-the-Month (ToM) Cash Flow**
*   **Rule:** Buy SPY or QQQ at the close on the 4th-to-last trading day of the month; sell at the close on the 3rd trading day of the new month.
*   **Inefficiency:** Structural cash flows. 401(k) contributions, automated payroll deductions, and portfolio rebalancing cluster around the end/beginning of the month, creating systematic buying pressure.
*   **Data Needed:** Daily ETF bars and trading calendar (to calculate end-of-month offset).
*   **Kill Test:** The remaining 14-16 trading days of the month generate a higher average return than the 7-day ToM window.

**Idea 5: Liquidity Vacuum (Inside Bar / NR7 Breakout)**
*   **Rule:** Buy if today's close > yesterday's high AND yesterday's high-to-low range was the narrowest of the last 7 days (NR7); hold 3 days.
*   **Inefficiency:** Volatility clustering. Markets alternate between range contraction (balance) and expansion (imbalance). 
*   **Data Needed:** Daily OHLC.
*   **Kill Test:** Entering on a random day yields the same or better average 3-day return as entering after an NR7 day.

### 2. Three Multi-Day Swing Ideas (Forex, H4/Daily Bars)

**Idea 6: Carry-Trend Alignment (Daily)**
*   **Rule:** Buy a currency pair IF the 100-day Simple Moving Average is sloping up AND the interest rate differential (base minus quote) is positive; exit if the 100 SMA slopes down.
*   **Inefficiency:** Yield-seeking institutional capital. Funds buy high-yielding currencies and sell low-yielding ones. Over time, this massive capital flow creates trends that overpower daily noise.
*   **Data Needed:** Daily FX prices, historical central bank interest rates.
*   **Kill Test:** Testing the exact inverse (buying negative yield pairs in downtrends) performs similarly, meaning the rate differential adds no alpha to a standard trend filter.

**Idea 7: The "Tuesday Reversal" Weekly Expansion (H4)**
*   **Rule:** If Monday and Tuesday create a confined range (High - Low < 1 weekly ATR), buy at Wednesday's open if price is > Tuesday's high; exit at Friday's close.
*   **Inefficiency:** Institutional week-start positioning. Big players often use Monday/Tuesday to establish macro positions for the week. The true directional move usually breaks out Wednesday and persists into the Friday close.
*   **Data Needed:** H4 OHLC, ATR.
*   **Kill Test:** Buying Monday open and holding to Friday close (baseline weekly drift) yields the same PF.

**Idea 8: Consecutive Closes Momentum (Daily)**
*   **Rule:** Buy after 3 consecutive higher daily closes where the 3rd close is in the top 20% of its daily range; hold for 5 days.
*   **Inefficiency:** Institutional scaling. Real buying pressure leaves footprints of relentless daily closes near the highs, punishing early short-sellers and forcing short-covering rallies.
*   **Data Needed:** Daily OHLC.
*   **Kill Test:** The 5-day forward return following 3 consecutive *lower* closes is mathematically equal (just inverted), proving it's random walk noise.

### 3. Published Anomalies & Expected OOS Decay

If you are moving to Daily bars, test these academically replicated anomalies first. 
1. **Time-Series Momentum (12-month minus 1-month):** Buy assets up over the last 12 months, skip the most recent 1 month (to avoid short-term reversal). 
   * *Decay:* Extremely slow. Relies on human behavioral herding. Performs well long-term but suffers brutal crashes at macro turning points (e.g., 2009).
2. **Post-Earnings Announcement Drift (PEAD):** (See Idea 1).
   * *Decay:* High in large-cap tech (algos price it instantly now). Medium/Low in small-to-mid caps. You must test this on your 24 mid/small cap universe.
3. **Turn-of-the-Month:** (See Idea 4).
   * *Decay:* Very low decay because the underlying inefficiency (when people get paid) hasn't changed. However, absolute returns are low without leverage.
4. **Short-Term Reversal (Volume spikes):** Buying a stock after a massive 1-2 day drop on high volume.
   * *Decay:* Massive. In academic papers, this looks like a money machine. Once you add bid/ask spread, slippage, and the reality that limit orders don't get filled unless the price plows through them, the edge usually vaporizes.
